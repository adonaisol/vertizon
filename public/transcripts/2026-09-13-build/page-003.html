Both are good additions, and they sharpen the design because they break a naive scorer in different ways:

  • Non-native English / hard-to-follow reasoning. The risk is the model scoring the manager's prose instead of the employee's evidence: clumsy writing about strong work gets marked down. That's a fairness bug, not a UX nit, and it's exactly the thing to test for. The planted truth for this manager: evidence is genuinely solid, only the writing is rough. If the extraction rates this team low, the scorer is broken.
  • Terse feedback. "Solid year. Meets." tells you nothing. The naive failure is treating no evidence as weak evidence and flagging the whole team as over-rated. So the extraction needs two separate outputs per employee: evidence strength and evidence sufficiency (how much the text actually supports any conclusion). Low-sufficiency cases show on the map as hollow points and the recommendation is "get more input from this manager", not "the rating is wrong".

That distinction, can't tell versus evidence is weak, is probably the single most important design decision in the tool, so thank you.

Section 2: Extraction (the model-as-rater step)

Input per employee: the rubric, the employee's level, the manager's written review, the manager's rating. Not the manager's identity or the other reviews, so the scorer can't be influenced by who wrote it.

Output (strict JSON, validated with zod): - evidence[]: each item has a verbatim quote (must appear in the review text, checked programmatically; anything not found is dropped and logged), a dimension, a level relative to the bar (below / at / above), and a one-line rationale. - strength (1–4): overall evidence-implied rating on the same scale as manager ratings, so the two are directly comparable. - sufficiency (low / medium / high): how much the text supports any conclusion at all. - notes: anything the model flagged, such as contradictory evidence or unclear writing.

Prompt design choices: the prompt says explicitly to judge the work described, not the writing quality, and to return low sufficiency rather than guess. Claude Sonnet 5 as the model, temperature 0.

Checks I'll run and commit (scripts/eval.ts, results in data/eval-report.md): 1. Stability: run extraction 3× per review, report how often strength changes. Also feeds the live "re-run" diff in the UI. 2. Quote fidelity: % of quotes that actually appear verbatim. 3. Planted-truth recovery: did the map surface the lenient/harsh managers and the planted individual cases? Did the non-native-English team score on evidence, not prose? Did the terse team come back low-sufficiency rather than low-strength?

If check 3 fails, I fix the prompt, not the data. The report shows what failed and what changed.

Live re-run path: same prompt, same validation, called from the browser with the in-memory key; result shown side by side with the bundled one.
